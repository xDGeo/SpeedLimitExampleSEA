02The problemGheorghe1:00

“Each situation gets the simplest tool that fits.”

  • Dark or icy: a neural network, because we have accident data.
  • Poor air: an LLM, because there is no data but it knows the EU limits.
  • Normal: a plain rule, 80 km/h. No AI where it is not needed.

“Three requirements were given; they left gaps, so we added five.”

  • REQ1 is the hard one: at most 1 near-accident per hour.
  • Each added one answers a ‘what if’: two reasons at once, how often, a failure, the operator disagrees, someone asks why.
05SpecificationsGheorghe0:50

“Specifications turn the requirements into exact numbers we can test.”

  • SPEC1–4 given, SPEC5–6 ours.
  • Two errors in the text: lux vs millilux (factor 1000), and ‘on average’ over what?
06DataGheorghe0:50

“One January of sensor readings and an accident log.”

  • We learn from near-accidents: 32 real accidents is too few.
  • Fixes: hours shifted by one, sensor type T used twice, an undocumented sensor, licence plates dropped.

→ Mihail: “Mihail shows how we built it.”

07ArchitectureMihail1:00

“The AI only proposes. Simple rules decide.”

  • Bad input is rejected and the current limit stays.
  • Router: dark or icy? Only then the network runs. The LLM always checks the air.
  • Decider takes the lowest proposal, otherwise 80. Any failure: safe limit plus operator review.
08Neural networkMihail1:00

“The speed limit is an input, so we can ask: how risky would each limit be?”

  • Nine inputs, output near-accidents per hour, asked for 40, 50, 60, 70 km/h.
  • Ten small networks: their disagreement is the confidence.
  • Trained with PyTorch on the 389 dark or icy hours, split by whole days.
09LLM for air qualityMihail0:50

“The LLM is boxed in on both sides.”

  • Only two checked numbers go in, so nothing can be injected.
  • Only 0, 10, 20, 30 or 40 km/h can come out.
  • Asked 3 times, majority wins. No valid answer: a formula decides plus review. Runs locally.
10Neural network resultsMihail1:00

“The network is no better than guessing the average.”

  • +0.4 % over 20 cross-validation runs: noise.
  • The counts behave like pure chance; same conditions next year give unrelated counts.
  • With a planted signal the same code learns it: the problem is the data, not the code.
11Requirements checkMihail1:15

“Everything is met and tested, except REQ1.”

  • 335 tests; each requirement has its own.
  • Every limit averages above 1 near-accident per hour in dark or icy hours.
  • So: fixed 60 km/h (most data, 230 h) plus operator review. ‘Lowest prediction’ chose 70 on icy nights.
12ConclusionMihail0:45

“Three lessons.”

  • Requirements first: they caught errors before the code.
  • Always a baseline: it showed honestly that the network has no skill.
  • Guard the AI: rules, fallbacks and a human keep it safe.
  • Next: clarify the Year N+1 file, define REQ1 precisely, run a speed-limit trial.

Likely questions

Why 60 km/h and not the lowest prediction?

No limit meets REQ1. ‘Lowest prediction’ chose 70 km/h on icy nights, because in the data lower limits come with more near-accidents: noise or confounding. 60 has the most evidence (230 h); 40 has only 7 h. An operator reviews it.

Slide 11 · appendix 27

If the network adds nothing, why keep it?

Today it does not change the outcome, and we say so. It stays so it can take over once a trial gives data with real signal.

Slide 10

How do you know it is the data and not your code?

Control test: with a planted signal in fake data, the same code learns it. And the counts behave like pure chance (variance = mean).

Slide 10

Can the LLM do something dangerous?

Only two checked numbers go in, only 0–40 km/h reductions come out, asked 3 times with a majority vote, every call logged. No valid answer: labelled formula plus review.

Slide 9 · appendix 19

What if a sensor fails?

Several sensors are combined by median; impossible readings are rejected; the current limit stays; it is logged and flagged (REQ6).

Slide 7

Why only near-accidents?

REQ1 is stated in near-accidents. January has 32 real accidents against about 960 near-accidents: too few to learn from.

Slide 6

Would you retrain on Year N+1?

Not yet. Its sensor readings are identical to Year N, yet near-accidents fell 964 → 822. Clarify the file first; retraining gave no skill either.

Appendix 26

Why not generate synthetic data?

We tested it: copies of noise add nothing, and data with an assumed speed effect only teaches the network our assumption.

Appendix 28

Is it ready to deploy?

As decision support, yes; as an autonomous system, no. REQ1 needs better data: a precise REQ1 and a controlled trial (about 260 dark or icy hours per limit).
