The one message

We built a working, well-tested system, but the data cannot teach it the effect of speed. It is ready as an advisor; automatic limits need a controlled trial first.

The talk follows the order we worked in (slide 2): requirements → data → design → tests and implementation → evaluation → conclusion. Each slide's kicker names its step, and the tags top right name the requirements it covers.

Likely questions

Short answers. If you don't know, say “we did not measure that” and offer to follow up.

GheorgheWhy not set 40 km/h whenever it is dark or icy? Surely that is safest.

We have only 7 hours of evidence at 40 km/h, and the data does not show that it helps. Research supports 40 on black ice in darkness, but not for darkness alone, and every cut costs travel time. That trade-off is what expert rules and a trial should settle.

Slides 4, 12 · backup 18

GheorgheIf the network adds nothing, why keep it?

It does not change the outcome today, and we say so. It stays in the design so it can take over if a trial produces data with a real signal. Keeping it costs little; removing it would mean rebuilding later.

Slides 9, 11

MihailWould a bigger model or deep learning do better?

No. A bigger network scored −0.7 % and a deeper one −0.8 %, and a linear model and gradient boosting did no better than a constant. The counts behave like pure chance (variance/mean 1.00). The limit is the data, not the model.

Slides 9, 10 · backup 17

GheorgheIs the fixed 60 km/h safe?

It is a cautious default, not a proven safe limit, which is why every such decision asks the operator to confirm. Research suggests 40 or 50 km/h on black ice, so on icy nights 60 is probably not strict enough.

Slide 11 · backup 18

GheorgheHow much operator work does the review flag create?

A lot, on this data: 389 of January's 744 hours are dark or icy, about 52 %, and all are flagged today. That is another reason to call it an advisor. Expert rules for clear-cut cases would reduce the load.

Slide 11 · backup 23

GheorgheHow long would the trial take?

About 1,050 dark or icy hours, roughly three Januaries on this one road, or less if several road sections run in parallel. We have not estimated the cost; that needs the operator's figures.

Slide 12

MihailWhat about the Year N+1 data?

Its sensor readings are identical to Year N, value for value, yet the near-accident counts differ and are unrelated (r = 0.003). A model retrained on it scores −1.1 %, so it does not change our conclusion.

Slide 10 · backup 19

MihailCan we trust the LLM? Why use one at all?

The assignment required an LLM with an API. We treat it as untrusted: numbers in, five legal answers out, three samples, a rule-based fallback and a review flag. Research found no evidence that cutting below 80 km/h for air alone helps, so a simple rule table could replace it.

Slide 8 · backup 18 · appendix 26

GheorgheIs it legal to deploy this?

It is high-risk under the EU AI Act. As an advisor with an operator in charge, the safeguards are built in. Setting limits automatically would first need representative data and proven accuracy (Art. 10 and 15) and a conformity assessment. This is our reading, not legal advice.

Backup 23

GheorgheWhat happens if a sensor fails?

Redundant sensors are combined by their median, so one faulty sensor cannot move the value. Impossible readings are rejected, the current limit is kept, and the cycle is logged. A failure never silently publishes a higher limit (REQ6).

Slide 6 · backup 16

MihailDid the AI write the code? How do you know it is right?

Claude Code ran the data analysis and drafted the UML and test cases; we checked, corrected and approved each step, then it implemented against them. 335 tests and an architecture test check the result, and a planted-signal control test shows the weak result comes from the data.

Slides 2, 6, 9 · backup 21 · appendix 25–26

GheorgheIs “at most one near-accident per hour” even realistic?

Probably not as written. Even clear hours average 1.33 per hour, above the target. REQ1 also needs a road section, an averaging period and a way to measure compliance.

Slide 12 · backup 15

MihailDoes the system store personal data?

Licence plates are dropped when the files are loaded. They never reach the features, the model or the logs, and a test checks that.

Slide 4 · backup 16

MihailWhat would the change requests cost?

Fahrenheit sensors and “only skidding counts” each touch one component, followed by retraining. Exempting electric cars is the large one: two limits through the Decider, GUI, log and the physical signs.
