The MVP works end to end and is engineered properly, but the data cannot support REQ1. Do not automate speed limits yet: fix the data and run a controlled trial, with operators in charge meanwhile.

09Features and networkGheorghe1:00

“A small model, trained only where it is used.”

  • 9 inputs: the speed limit (what we control), log illuminance, water, temperature, wind, humidity, traffic, dark and ice flags; medians of redundant sensors.
  • Left out: minute/second, noise dB, wind direction, licence plates.
  • Output: near-accidents per hour for each candidate limit, 40 to 70 km/h.
  • 10 networks, 9→16→8→1, Poisson loss; 389 dark or icy hours; split by day plus 20-fold cross-validation.

→ Mihail: “So, does it work? Mihail.”

10Network resultMihail1:15

“It does not beat guessing the average.”

  • Skill = how much better than always predicting the average.
  • One split said +1.7 %; 20 folds say +0.4 %, ranging from −3.2 to +4.7: noise.
  • Deviance 0.982 vs 0.999 vs 0.978: the simple linear model is even slightly better.
  • Medium confidence needs +2 %, so confidence is always Low, and the GUI says so.

Stress: not a bug. A planted-signal control test shows the same code learns when a signal exists.

→ Gheorghe: “What that means for REQ1.”

11REQ1 fallbackGheorghe1:15

“No limit meets REQ1, so the system falls back to 60 km/h and asks the operator.”

  • Replayed all 389 dark or icy hours: 0 meet REQ1.
  • First rule, “lowest prediction”: 70 km/h in every hour, icy nights included. The network learned the backwards pattern.
  • Current rule: highest limit at or below 1/h, otherwise a fixed 60 km/h plus review; 60 has the most evidence (230 h).
  • Honest: today the network does not change the outcome. It stays ready for better data.

12LLM for air qualityMihail1:00

“The LLM is fenced in: numbers in, one of five legal values out.”

  • Open-Meteo forecast, worst PM10 and NO₂ of the next 6 hours; input guardrail allows numbers only.

13Research vs. systemGheorghe1:00

“Research would go slower than 60 on ice, and would not cut for darkness or air alone.”

  • The research advisor uses cited stopping-distance physics and crash models; it never sets the limit.
  • Ice: 40 (dark), 50 (day). Our 60 is not cautious enough.

18Lessons learnedMihail1:00

“Six lessons, one line each.”

  • Baseline first: 0.982 means nothing until a constant scores 0.999.
  • One split misleads: +1.7 % became +0.4 %.
  • Run a control: planted signal = code or data?
  • Check the joins: one hour off creates or hides signal.
  • Guard weak models: “lowest prediction” chose 70 km/h on ice.
  • Data needs information: synthetic data returns assumptions.

If time: requirement problems show up in the data; test-first caught real bugs.

Likely questions

Short answers. If you don't know, say “we did not measure that” and offer to follow up.

GheorgheWhy not set 40 km/h whenever it is dark or icy? Surely that is safest.

We have only 7 hours of evidence at 40 km/h, and the data does not show that it helps. Research does support 40 on black ice in darkness, but not for darkness alone, and every cut costs travel time. That trade-off is exactly what the expert rules and the trial should settle.

Slides 6, 13, 19

GheorgheIf the network adds nothing, why keep it?

It does not change the outcome today, and we say so. It stays in the design so that it can take over if a trial produces data with real signal. Keeping it costs little; removing it would mean rebuilding later.

Slides 10, 11

MihailWould a bigger model or deep learning do better?

No. A linear model and gradient boosting do no better than a constant either. The counts behave like pure randomness (variance/mean 1.00), and identical conditions in two years give uncorrelated counts (r = 0.003). The limit is the data, not the model.

Slides 6, 10

GheorgheIs the fixed 60 km/h safe?

It is a cautious default, not a proven safe limit. That is why every such decision asks the operator to review it. Research suggests 40 or 50 km/h on black ice, so on icy nights 60 is probably not strict enough.

Slides 11, 13

GheorgheHow much operator work does the review flag create?

A lot, on this data: 389 of January's 744 hours are dark or icy, about 52 %, and all of them are flagged today. That is another reason not to call this automation yet. Expert rules for clear-cut cases would reduce the load.

Slides 11, 17

GheorgheHow long would the trial take, and what would it cost?

About 1,050 dark or icy hours, roughly three Januaries on this one road, or less if several road sections run in parallel. We have not estimated the cost; that needs the operator's figures for signs, staff and data collection.

Slide 19

MihailWhy is the Year N+1 file identical to Year N?

We don't know. A real year cannot repeat value for value, so it is most likely a copy. Finding out is our first recommendation, because the answer decides whether retraining makes sense at all.

Slide 14

MihailCan we trust the LLM? Why use one at all?

The assignment required an LLM with an API. We treat it as untrusted: numbers in, five legal answers out, three samples, a rule-based fallback and a review flag. Research found no evidence that cutting below 80 km/h for air alone helps, so a simple rule table could replace it.

Slides 12, 13 · appendix slide 24

GheorgheIs it legal to deploy this?

It is high-risk under the EU AI Act. Data governance (Art. 10) and accuracy (Art. 15) are not met today, and a conformity assessment is still to do. So not in its current state. This is our reading, not legal advice, and other countries need their own check.

Slide 17

GheorgheWhat happens if a sensor fails?

Redundant sensors are combined by their median, so one faulty sensor cannot move the value. Impossible readings are rejected, the current limit is kept, and the cycle is logged. A failure never silently publishes a higher limit (REQ6).

Slides 5, 8

MihailHow do you know the code is right when the model is weak?

335 automated tests, at least five per component, including exact boundary tests and an architecture test. A control experiment plants a known signal, and the same code learns it, so the weak result comes from the data.

Slides 10, 16 · appendix slides 23–24

GheorgheIs “at most one near-accident per hour” even realistic?

Probably not as written. Even clear hours average 1.33 per hour, above the target. REQ1 also needs a road section, an averaging period and a way to measure compliance. That is recommendation 2.

Slides 4, 19

MihailDoes the system store personal data?

Licence plates are dropped when the files are loaded. They never reach the features, the model or the logs, and a test checks that.

Slide 5

MihailWhat would the change requests cost?

Fahrenheit sensors and “only skidding counts” each touch one component, followed by retraining. Exempting electric cars is the large one: two limits through the Decider, GUI, log and the physical signs.
