  • No valid answer: rule-based fallback plus a review flag.

14Results · The networkGheorghe0:50

“The network predicts no better than the average.”

  • One split said +1.7 %; 20 folds say +0.4 %, between −3.2 and +4.7: noise.
  • Why: counts behave like pure chance (1.00), and identical conditions in two years give unrelated counts.
  • Control test: a planted signal is learned, so the code works and the data is the limit.

→ Mihail: “Mihail goes back to the requirements.”

15Results · Requirements checkMihail0:55

“Every requirement is met and tested, except REQ1.”

  • Go down the list: REQ2 to REQ8 and privacy each have passing tests.
  • Chart: every limit is above 1 near-accident per hour. 40 km/h has only 7 hours: noise or confounding, not proof that slower is riskier.
  • So in every dark or icy hour: 60 km/h and an operator review. “That is the 60 from the title.”

→ Gheorghe: “And what about next year's data?”

16Results · RetrainingGheorghe0:45

“Do not retrain: the Year N+1 sensor file repeats last year.”

  • Drift is exactly zero on every sensor, yet near-accidents fell from 964 to 822.
  • Neither the old nor a retrained model has skill.
  • First find out why the file repeats Year N.

17Wrap-up · Use of AIMihail0:35

“Ourselves first, then prompts.”

  • Course rule: without AI, initial prompt, improved prompt.
  • Initial prompts hid bad decisions, such as the “lowest prediction” rule that picked 70 km/h on icy nights.
  • Improved prompts: our files as context, short and testable answers.
18Wrap-up · ConclusionMihail0:45

“A sound system, on data that cannot carry REQ1.”

  • Three lessons: requirements first, always a baseline, guard weak models.
  • Next: explain the Year N+1 file, define REQ1 precisely, run a trial of about 260 dark or icy hours per limit.
  • Until then, operators decide, supported by the system.

Likely questions

Short answers. If you don't know, say “we did not measure that” and offer to follow up.

MihailWhere do the added requirements come from?

From the stakeholders: drivers give REQ1 and REQ3, operators REQ7, police and regulators REQ8, maintenance REQ6, and the data protection officer the privacy requirement.

Slides 4, 5 · appendix 20–21

GheorgheWhy not set 40 km/h whenever it is dark or icy?

There are only 7 hours of evidence at 40 km/h, and the data does not show that it helps. Published research supports 40 on black ice in darkness, but not for darkness alone. That trade-off is for expert rules and a trial.

Slide 15 · appendix 30

GheorgheIf the network adds nothing, why keep it?

Today it does not change the outcome, and we say so. It stays in the design so that it can take over once a trial gives data with real signal.

Slides 14, 15 · appendix 29

GheorgheWould a bigger model do better?

No. A linear model does no better than a constant either. The counts behave like pure chance, so the limit is the data, not the model.

Slide 14

GheorgheIs traffic an input, or something to optimise?

We use it as an input because it describes the conditions. Optimising throughput would be a new requirement with its own trade-off against safety; the case study does not ask for it.

Slide 11

GheorgheWhy count only near-accidents and not real accidents?

REQ1 is stated in near-accidents (SPEC4). January has only 32 real accidents against about 960 near-accidents, too few to learn from. A combined danger index would be a change to REQ1.

Slides 5, 6

MihailCan we trust the LLM?

We don't trust it blindly: numbers in, five allowed answers out, three samples, a fallback and a review flag. All 5 golden cases are in range.

Slide 12 · appendix 23

GheorgheWhy is the Year N+1 file identical to Year N?

We don't know. A real year cannot repeat value for value, so it is most likely a copy. Finding out comes first, because it decides whether retraining makes sense.

Slide 16

GheorgheWhat happens if a sensor fails?

Redundant sensors are combined by median, impossible readings are rejected, the current limit is kept, and the cycle is logged. A failure never silently raises the limit (REQ6).

Slides 8, 9

MihailIs it legal to deploy this?

It is high-risk under the EU AI Act. Data governance (Art. 10) and accuracy (Art. 15) are not met today. This is our reading, not legal advice.

Appendix 32

MihailWhat would the change requests cost?

Fahrenheit sensors and “only skidding counts” each touch one component, then retraining. Exempting electric cars is large: two limits through the Decider, GUI, log and the signs.
